> 6.522 There is indeed that which cannot be put into words. It shows itself; it is the mystical.

My memory is poor. I would like to give you specific examples of things I have failed to remember, but I cannot even remember those. Conversations with someone, violence, debts—all disappear after a night’s sleep. Only abstractions remain in my head. As a child, I would fall asleep thinking: why am I living through a present I will not even remember? If only I could open my eyes and find myself in a happy future. Come to think of it, thoughts I have had at some point seem less easily erased. The trouble, of course, is that thoughts are nothing concrete.

On August 28, 2016, I wrote this on my blog: “Question: A memory you never want to forget as long as you live? / Answer: My first meeting with Illya.” In 2016, it had not been long since I first met Illya. That must be why I still remembered our first meeting. I have forgotten it completely now, though.

This, too, is one of the sad things that happen when your memory is poor. Yet I can bear forgetting precious things. Of course it hurts to have forgotten my first meeting with the person I love, but I can meet Illya again anytime, anywhere, even right this moment. What I truly cannot bear are the x’s that have left before there was even time to speak of them. No one can speak of them, and no one even tries; yet each must be a vision longer than *In Search of Lost Time*, more beautiful than *Spring and Asura*.

They are clouds, trees, waves. People look at clouds and say they can talk about clouds. Yet even as they look, all they talk about are imaginary fish, imaginary dogs, even UFOs; of clouds themselves they say nothing at all. It is the same with trees. People liken the shapes of cracks etched into a tree, or the branching of its limbs, to other concepts, and say they can talk about trees. Yet none of this is talk about trees. With waves, things are even worse. People talk about the rocking of a boat on the waves, or, worse still, look only at the sea and never so much as glance at the waves.

No one can speak of clouds, trees, waves. Yet they are there; they even appear to us. Still, I cannot give a name to even one cloud, one tree, one wave. All I can utter are exclamations. It is this powerlessness of memory that I truly cannot bear.

If a day comes when my memory is good enough—if it ever does—I intend to read a single cloud in place of a poem by Baek Seok, to quote a tree I met yesterday in place of the Gilbert Simondon I read yesterday, and, in place of *Last Year at Marienbad*, to gaze endlessly at the quiet waves off Wido in Buan County.
